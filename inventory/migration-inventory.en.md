New SQL validation workflow: `instructions/sql-validation-workflow.en.md`. Every formal SQL task follows a seven-stage proof chain: result contract, data facts, risk profile, implementation and static review, independent proof, delivery reconciliation, and recurrence control. It checks nine fixed dimensions: definition, population, grain, relationships, time, units, statistics, delivery, and reproducibility. Every formal SQL task must also retain a completed validation record based on `instructions/templates/sql-validation-record.en.md`; without one, its status may only be draft or pending validation. The risk profile determines where deeper evidence is required; it cannot waive definition, grain, independent recomputation, or delivery reconciliation. The eight validation gates cover data availability; schema and semantics; grain and uniqueness; join cardinality and row multiplication; metric invariants; independent recomputation and reconciliation; boundaries and business spot checks; and delivery/runtime checks. Failures must be fixed; an unrun high-risk check cannot be labeled verified; and a recurring error must become an executable check or minimal counterexample. Statistical outputs must also generate p-value stars and significance labels programmatically, verify DID sign and units, and reconcile report, XLSX, CSV, script output, and query result. PSM/DID must retain match rate, maximum SMD, pre/post windows, and parallel-trend status.
